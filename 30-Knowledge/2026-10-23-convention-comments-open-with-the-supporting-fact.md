## The rule

A comment written in the user's name that backs someone's post does not open with an agreement word:
"Agree", "Agreed", "Exactly", "Totally agree", "So true", "100%", or the same in any other language.
The support comes from the fact itself, framed as proof of the author's point: a figure, a place, a
date, a case the user knows.

## Why

An instruction such as "support the author's thesis" turns, after a few runs, into every comment
opening with "Agree,". Read one after another on the user's profile, they look written by a bot, which
is the opposite of what a comment is for. A fact that confirms the point supports it more and sounds
like a person who knows the subject.

## How

- Start with the fact: "Same pattern at our branch library: loans of board games doubled once they
  were shelved by the entrance."
- Vary the shape between comments: some open with a place, some with a number, some with a date, some
  with a short question.
- When comments are written by a routine, check the opener in code (a small list of refused first
  words, in every language the routine writes) rather than trusting the prompt.
- The rest of the writing rules still apply: [[2026-09-10-convention-write-like-a-person]].

## Example

An invented post says small gyms keep members by knowing their names. Instead of "Agree, community is
everything", write "Our front desk greets every member by name and renewals went from 61% to 78% in a
year."
