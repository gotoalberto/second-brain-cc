## The rule

Ask one question per turn when the answer may be uncomfortable for whoever answers: when it could
reveal a gap, contradict something said earlier, or separate having built something from actually
using it.

## Why

Faced with a compound question ("What is your experience with X? Do you use it yourself? Did you
build something on it?"), people answer the part that is easiest to defend and treat the whole
question as answered. If nobody comes back to the untouched part, the gap is never recorded, not
because anyone lied but because they never had to face it. Asked on its own, the same question gets
a direct answer.

## How to apply

- In interviews, reviews and status checks, split the uncomfortable part into its own question.
- If a compound question was already asked and only one part was answered, do not assume the rest is
  implied. Ask it again on its own before drawing a conclusion.
- The same applies when an agent asks the user something: one decision per question when the options
  are not obvious.
