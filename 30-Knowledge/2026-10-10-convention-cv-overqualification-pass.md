## The rule

Every CV is checked against the level of the role before it goes out, and its emphasis is adjusted so
the reader sees someone who fits the role. The pass works both ways:

- **The role carries responsibility over people** (manager, lead, head, director; direct reports,
  hiring, a budget): the team goes up front. How many people report to the user and where, that they
  hire, who they report to. The "what I bring" block opens with leading teams.
- **The role carries none** (specialist, analyst, individual contributor): the overqualification
  pass below, so the CV is not set aside as aimed above the role.

In both directions nothing is removed or softened and nothing is invented: titles, positions and
dates are facts. Only order and emphasis move. Say in the reply which way the CV was pitched and
which edits were made; the user decides whether that is the right level.

## The overqualification pass

Three edits and nothing more:

1. **Reorder the "what I bring" block** so it opens with hands-on delivery that matches the role, not
   with team leadership or scale.
2. **State the reporting line once**, in the experience entry where it is true, and take it out of
   the summary. Saying it twice, once at the top, is what makes a CV read as aimed above the role.
3. **Leave every title and every date untouched.** Moving or softening a "Principal" or an
   "Engineering Manager" title would break the rule against inventing anything.

## Example

An invented candidate who runs an engineering group of about twenty people and reports to the VP of
Engineering of a ticketing company:

- For a *Senior Backend Engineer* posting at a booking platform, the summary opens with the services
  they still design and ship, the "reports to the VP" line moves into the current job's entry only,
  and the group size stays in that entry as a fact.
- For a *Head of Engineering* posting at a fitness app, the summary opens with the group they built
  and lead, how many engineers they hired, and the reporting line.

Both CVs carry the same titles and dates.
