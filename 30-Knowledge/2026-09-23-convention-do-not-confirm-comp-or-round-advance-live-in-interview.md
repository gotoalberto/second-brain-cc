## The rule

When the user interviews candidates, **neither a move to the next round nor a compensation figure is
confirmed in the interview itself** unless it has already been agreed with the rest of the hiring loop.
It is written down as a standing risk because it happened more than once, in two different shapes.

## The pattern

1. **Round advance.** A candidate was told on the screening call that they were moving to the
   technical round, before being compared against the rest of the pipeline. It had to be reversed
   days later, and because the promised next steps email never went out, the candidate's first written
   message from the process was the rejection.
2. **Compensation.** Twice, a candidate's number was answered with something like "I think we can
   cover that" before the band was settled, while the level of the role itself was still under
   discussion. Both times it had to be walked back.

## Why it keeps happening

Screenings run alone and fast, and they are the moment the candidate asks most directly whether both
sides are aligned. The natural answer under that pressure is a live yes. But neither decision belongs
to one interviewer: advancing depends on a comparison that has not happened yet mid screening, and pay
depends on a band that may itself still be moving.

## How to apply

- **Round advance**: say the next step will be confirmed in writing. Never announce it on the call.
- **Compensation**: acknowledge the number without confirming it can be met. "Noted, I'll check it
  against the band" costs nothing; "we can cover that" is a commitment made without the authority to
  keep it.
- **Units first.** If the candidate's figure may be in different units from the band (base against
  fully loaded cost, gross against net, yearly against monthly), do not react to it at all until the
  units are reconciled.
- When helping prepare an interview, put these two lines in the prep notes.
