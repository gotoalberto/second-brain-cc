## The rule

Before writing a **negative fact about a third party** (a candidate, a supplier, anyone
outside the conversation) whose source is a memory, a summary or a passing remark, verify it
in the primary source: the thread, the transcript, the calendar.

For neutral or positive facts the bar can be lower. For facts that can harm someone it is
not: these notes are later used to decide about real people, and an error spreads.

**Verify a correction to the same standard.** A correction feels like rigour and so gets
less scrutiny, which is exactly when it slips through. Reconstructing a fact from a partial
artefact and taking it over the account of someone who was present is the same mistake with
the sign flipped. When the user witnessed the event, their account outranks a reconstruction
from messages or logs; the artefacts add detail, they do not overrule.

## Corollary

If a negative assessment of someone already exists and a new data point "confirms" it, be
more sceptical, not less. Confirmation bias makes it easy to accept without checking.

## How it went wrong

A remark that someone missed a meeting was written into their profile as a no-show and
stacked on an earlier negative signal. A message thread suggested the opposite, so the note
was corrected. Then it turned out the no-show had been real, and the correction had to be
reverted. Each step trusted a partial source over the person who was there.
